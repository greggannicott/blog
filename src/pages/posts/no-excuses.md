Possibly the greatest words of advice I've been given were "no excuses".

I was considering taking up running and discussing it with my son's football coach. He said to me that what he does is tells himself that he's going to run 3 times a week, no excuses.

Weather a bit cold or wet? Doesn't matter.
Not quite "feeling it" this week? Doesn't matter.
Would rather do something else? Doesn't matter.

The only exceptions to the rule is illness or if you genuinely do not have the time.

I've followed that advice and I firmly believe it is what has kept me running, and it turns out I bloody love running. For the past 6 years I've run at least 3 times a week. Generally speaking it is more. There are times where I've gone weeks without running due to injury or illness, but as soon as I can I get back on it.

There are a couple things I love about the no excuses approach. As with most things, I go through phases of whether I enjoy and care about running. There are times where improving my pace or increasing my distance is a big focus of mine. I can't wait to get back out there and push it. There are other times it just doesn't matter. By running on a regular basis by body is always in a position - it always has a level of fitness - that when I do care, I can act on my enthusiasm.

The other key benefit is that by just turning up, you tend to naturally improve. So even if you aren't feeling it this week, it all contributes to improvement.

For a long time I used and loved this approach to running. I'd tell anyone that would listen about the benefits of it. But I never really applied it elsewhere. Then at the start of this year I began to make efforts to improve my life. No doubt some of those efforts will appear in subsequent blog posts, but one thing I wanted to do was just complete something and get it out there.

So I decided to take the no excuses approach. Every week I would dedicate one hour a week to working on a blog. I've made many failed attempts to starting a blog, but I figured if I doing it every week, I'd eventually get to a point where I'd have a blog. There will be weeks where I really don't want to have to work on the blog, but there would be others where it would be fantastic to have an outlet for my thoughts. By dedicating an hour a week I'd essentially keep up a level of fitness from a blogging point of view, and be in a position to enjoy writing posts when I'm really feeling it.
